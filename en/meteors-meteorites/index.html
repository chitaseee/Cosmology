Meteors and Meteorites: Differences and Origins

A meteor is a streak of light from space material entering an atmosphere. If part reaches the ground, it is a meteorite, preserving clues to Solar System history.

Educational guide · Reviewed: 8 November 2026

In brief: A meteor is a streak of light from space material entering an atmosphere. If part reaches the ground, it is a meteorite, preserving clues to Solar System history.

Key properties and structure

A small body in space is a meteoroid. During rapid atmospheric entry, heating and interactions with air produce light. Meteorites can originate from asteroids, the Moon or Mars.

Scientific processes and evidence

A meteor shower occurs when Earth passes through debris left by a comet or sometimes an asteroid. The trails appear to radiate from a point in the sky called the radiant.

Why it matters for astronomy

Research on Meteors and Meteorites: Differences and Origins combines telescope observations, spacecraft measurements and physical models. Every conclusion must be interpreted with the uncertainties and limits of the available data.

What does this topic teach us about the universe?
