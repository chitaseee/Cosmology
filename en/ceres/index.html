Ceres: The Dwarf Planet in the Asteroid Belt

Ceres is the largest object in the asteroid belt and a dwarf planet. Salts, craters and evidence of briny water reveal its complex history.

Educational guide · Reviewed: 8 November 2026

In brief: Ceres is the largest object in the asteroid belt and a dwarf planet. Salts, craters and evidence of briny water reveal its complex history.

Key properties and structure

Ceres lies between Mars and Jupiter and its gravity makes it nearly spherical. The bright spots in Occator crater are largely salt deposits left after briny water reached the surface and evaporated.

Scientific processes and evidence

NASA’s Dawn spacecraft mapped Ceres’ gravity, surface and composition, revealing a more complex geological history than that of a simple space rock.

Why it matters for astronomy

Research on Ceres: The Dwarf Planet in the Asteroid Belt combines telescope observations, spacecraft measurements and physical models. Every conclusion must be interpreted with the uncertainties and limits of the available data.

Frequently asked question

What does this topic teach us about the universe?

It helps scientists understand the physical and historical processes that shape celestial objects. Scientific interpretation must rely on observations and account for the limits of each method.
