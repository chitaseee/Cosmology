How Astronomers Detect Exoplanets

Astronomers use methods such as transits, radial velocity, direct imaging and microlensing to discover planets around distant stars.

Educational guide · Reviewed: 8 November 2026

In brief: Astronomers use methods such as transits, radial velocity, direct imaging and microlensing to discover planets around distant stars.

Key properties and structure

In the transit method, a star’s brightness dips slightly as a planet crosses it. Radial-velocity measurements detect a star’s wobble through shifts in spectral lines.

Scientific processes and evidence

Direct imaging suppresses starlight so a faint planet can be seen. Gravitational microlensing can reveal worlds that are difficult to find by other methods.

Why it matters for astronomy

Research on How Astronomers Detect Exoplanets combines telescope observations, spacecraft measurements and physical models. Every conclusion must be interpreted with the uncertainties and limits of the available data.

Frequently asked question

What does this topic teach us about the universe?

It helps scientists understand the physical and historical processes that shape celestial objects. Scientific interpretation must rely on observations and account for the limits of each method.
