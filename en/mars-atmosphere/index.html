Mars’s Atmosphere: Why the Red Planet Is Cold and Dry

Mars has a very thin atmosphere made mostly of carbon dioxide. Low pressure and the absence of a strong global magnetic field contribute to its cold, dry surface.

Educational guide · Reviewed: 8 November 2026

In brief: Mars has a very thin atmosphere made mostly of carbon dioxide. Low pressure and the absence of a strong global magnetic field contribute to its cold, dry surface.

Key properties and structure

Mars’s surface pressure is less than one percent of Earth’s, so liquid water is unstable under most surface conditions. Water ice exists at the poles and underground, while winds can raise dust storms.

Scientific processes and evidence

Mars lacks a global magnetic field like Earth’s. The solar wind has helped remove part of its atmosphere over time; NASA’s MAVEN mission studies this process.

Why it matters for astronomy

Research on Mars’s Atmosphere: Why the Red Planet Is Cold and Dry combines telescope observations, spacecraft measurements and physical models. Every conclusion must be interpreted with the uncertainties and limits of the available data.
